Pain & Fever

Medicines people commonly know for easing pain and bringing down fever.

Remember: this is not a doctor's prescription. The guides below are general information only. Your doctor or pharmacist decides what is right for you.

Pain & Fever

Paracetamol

Active ingredient: Paracetamol

Generally associated with the temporary relief of pain and the reduction of fever.

In simple words

It is thought to work mainly in the brain, where it lowers the signals that make us feel pain and raise body temperature.

Who can take it & how often
Age groups
Adults, and children using child-specific products. For children the amount depends on weight and the product, so follow the label. Babies need a doctor's advice first.
How often
Only when needed. The label tells the gap needed between doses and the daily maximum. Never take extra doses.
Prescription
Usually available without a prescription.
Be careful
Many cold and flu products also contain paracetamol. Check labels so you do not take it twice.

General information only. Always follow the product label or your doctor's instructions.

Pain & Inflammation

Ibuprofen

Active ingredient: Ibuprofen

Belongs to a group called NSAIDs. Generally associated with easing pain, swelling and fever.

In simple words

It blocks enzymes (called COX) that make the chemicals which cause pain and swelling.

Who can take it & how often
Age groups
Adults and older children using suitable products. For children the amount depends on weight and age, so follow the label. Not for babies unless a doctor says so.
How often
Only when needed, with gaps between doses as written on the label. It is usually taken with or after food.
Prescription
Many forms are sold without a prescription. Stronger forms need one.
Be careful
People with stomach ulcers, kidney problems or asthma, and pregnant women, should ask a doctor first.

General information only. Always follow the product label or your doctor's instructions.

Pain & Blood

Aspirin

Active ingredient: Acetylsalicylic acid

Also an NSAID. Generally associated with pain relief and with affecting how blood platelets stick together.

In simple words

It blocks the same COX enzymes, and this also makes platelets in the blood less sticky.

Who can take it & how often
Age groups
Adults. Children and teenagers should not take it unless a doctor says so, because of a rare but serious illness called Reye's syndrome.
How often
For pain, only when needed with gaps as written on the label. Daily use for the heart is a different matter and is decided only by a doctor.
Prescription
The pain-relief form is sold without a prescription. Daily use for the heart should only be on a doctor's advice.
Be careful
It can irritate the stomach. People with ulcers, bleeding problems or asthma should ask a doctor first.

General information only. Always follow the product label or your doctor's instructions.
