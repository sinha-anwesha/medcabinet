Allergy & Breathing

Medicines linked with allergy symptoms and with keeping the airways open.

Remember: this is not a doctor's prescription. The guides below are general information only. Your doctor or pharmacist decides what is right for you.

Antiallergy

Cetirizine

Active ingredient: Cetirizine hydrochloride

An antihistamine. Generally associated with easing allergy symptoms such as sneezing and itching.

In simple words

In an allergy the body releases histamine. Cetirizine sits on the histamine receptors so histamine cannot cause itching and a runny nose.

Who can take it & how often
Age groups
Adults and older children. Younger children should only use child products as shown on the label. Ask a doctor for toddlers.
How often
Usually once a day when allergy symptoms are present. Follow the label.
Prescription
Often sold without a prescription.
Be careful
It can make some people sleepy, so be careful when driving or using machines.

General information only. Always follow the product label or your doctor's instructions.

Breathing

Salbutamol

Active ingredient: Salbutamol

A reliever for the airways. It helps the tubes in the lungs open wider so breathing feels easier.

In simple words

It attaches to receptors on the muscles around the airways and tells those muscles to relax.

Who can take it & how often
Age groups
Adults and children. The doctor chooses the type of inhaler or medicine.
How often
Used when breathing is difficult, or as the doctor has planned. If it is needed more often than usual, see a doctor quickly.
Prescription
Usually needs a doctor's prescription.
Be careful
If breathing gets worse or the medicine does not help, get medical help urgently.

General information only. Always follow the product label or your doctor's instructions.
