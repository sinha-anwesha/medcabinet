Long-term Conditions

Medicines for conditions that a doctor manages over a long time: blood sugar, cholesterol and thyroid.

Remember: this is not a doctor's prescription. The guides below are general information only. Your doctor or pharmacist decides what is right for you.

Diabetes

Metformin

Active ingredient: Metformin hydrochloride

Generally associated with managing blood sugar in type 2 diabetes.

In simple words

It reduces the sugar made by the liver and helps the body's cells use sugar better.

Who can take it & how often
Age groups
Adults, and some older children only under a specialist doctor.
How often
Taken regularly every day, usually with meals, exactly as the doctor says. It is not a 'when needed' medicine.
Prescription
Prescription only.
Be careful
Doctors check how the kidneys are working. Never stop or change it without asking.

General information only. Always follow the product label or your doctor's instructions.

Cholesterol

Atorvastatin

Active ingredient: Atorvastatin calcium

A statin. Generally associated with lowering cholesterol in the blood.

In simple words

It blocks an enzyme that the liver uses to make cholesterol.

Who can take it & how often
Age groups
Adults. Children only in special cases under a specialist doctor.
How often
Usually once a day at the same time every day, for a long time, as the doctor says.
Prescription
Prescription only.
Be careful
Tell the doctor about unexplained muscle pain. It is not for use in pregnancy.

General information only. Always follow the product label or your doctor's instructions.

Thyroid

Levothyroxine

Active ingredient: Levothyroxine sodium

A man-made form of a thyroid hormone. Generally associated with replacing the hormone when the thyroid makes too little.

In simple words

It does the same job as the body's own thyroid hormone, which controls how fast the body uses energy.

Who can take it & how often
Age groups
Adults and children. The doctor sets the amount using blood tests. Babies and children are looked after by a specialist.
How often
Usually once a day at the same time, often on an empty stomach, as the doctor says.
Prescription
Prescription only.
Be careful
Regular blood tests are needed. Do not stop or change it on your own.

General information only. Always follow the product label or your doctor's instructions.
