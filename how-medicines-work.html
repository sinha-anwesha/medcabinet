How Medicines Work

A medicine does not work by magic. It follows a path inside the body. Here is the basic path in five steps.

  1. The medicine

    This is the tablet, syrup or injection you see. It contains the active ingredient plus other substances that hold it together.

  2. The active ingredient

    This is the part that does the job. For example, the active ingredient of a paracetamol tablet is paracetamol.

  3. The biological target

    The active ingredient reaches a target in the body, such as a protein, an enzyme or a receptor on a cell. Think of the target as a lock and the active ingredient as a key.

  4. The cellular response

    When the key meets the lock, the cell changes what it does. It may switch something on, switch something off, or slow something down.

  5. The basic effect

    Many cells changing together create an effect we notice, such as less pain, lower fever or easier breathing.

This is a simple introduction. Real medicines are more complicated, and the same medicine can act differently in different people. Always ask a doctor or pharmacist about your own health.
