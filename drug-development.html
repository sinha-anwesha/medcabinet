Drug Development

A medicine goes through many research, testing and review stages before it reaches a pharmacy. This usually takes many years.

  1. Discovery

    Scientists study a disease and look for a target in the body. They then look for substances that might act on that target.

  2. Laboratory research

    Promising substances are tested in the lab and improved. The aim is to find one that works well and can be made reliably.

  3. Preclinical testing

    Before any human takes it, the substance is tested in cells and in animals to check basic safety and how it behaves in a living body.

  4. Clinical trials

    Volunteers take part in carefully planned studies, starting with small groups and moving to larger ones. Researchers check safety and whether the medicine really works.

  5. Regulatory review

    All the results are sent to a health authority. Experts check the evidence on safety, quality and benefit.

  6. Approval

    If the review is successful, the medicine is approved for specific uses. Its safety continues to be watched after it reaches the public.
