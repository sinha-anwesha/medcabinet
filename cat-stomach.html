Stomach & Digestion

Medicines linked with stomach acid and the movement of the gut.

Remember: this is not a doctor's prescription. The guides below are general information only. Your doctor or pharmacist decides what is right for you.

Stomach acid

Omeprazole

Active ingredient: Omeprazole

Reduces the amount of acid made by the stomach lining. Generally associated with acidity and heartburn.

In simple words

The stomach lining has tiny acid pumps. Omeprazole switches many of them off.

Who can take it & how often
Age groups
Mainly adults. Children only when a doctor advises.
How often
Usually once a day, often before food, for a limited time set by the label or the doctor.
Prescription
Some forms are sold for short-term use. Others need a prescription.
Be careful
If acidity lasts more than about two weeks, or comes with weight loss, trouble swallowing or vomiting, see a doctor.

General information only. Always follow the product label or your doctor's instructions.

Digestive

Loperamide

Active ingredient: Loperamide hydrochloride

Generally associated with loose motions. It slows the movement of the gut.

In simple words

It attaches to receptors in the gut wall that tell the intestine to move more slowly.

Who can take it & how often
Age groups
Adults and older children only. It is not for young children. Ask a doctor first for any child.
How often
Short-term use only, as written on the label.
Prescription
Rules differ from country to country. Ask a pharmacist.
Be careful
Drinking enough fluids is very important. See a doctor if there is fever or blood in the stool.

General information only. Always follow the product label or your doctor's instructions.
